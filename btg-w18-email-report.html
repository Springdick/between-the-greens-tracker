Between the Greens · 2026 Season Recap · Confirmed vs Joe Bronson's final standings (8/25)
Eighteen Tuesdays, six shooters, 210 cards and 5,250 clay targets called for. We broke 4,189 of them.

The season ran backwards in the two classes, and that's the whole story. Singles opened cold — three straight losses out of the gate — and finished 4–2 over the last six weeks to land at 9–9 and fourth place, level on wins with Gillen's Gunners. Handicap did the exact opposite: 4–2 out of the blocks, including a 113 in week two that stood as our best Handicap card of the entire season, then 3–9 the rest of the way to finish 7–11, sixth. One class found its feet as the other lost them. Split the difference and you get a team that was never out of it and never quite in it.

Three perfect cards. Bill broke the seal in W13 with the first clean 25 of the year. Brad answered with one in W16 — and then did it again in W17, back-to-back 25s, the only such pair anyone on this team has managed. Joe highlighted all three in yellow on his own sheet, which is the closest thing this league has to a plaque.

Bill Curtis finished top of the team in both classes. Singles 401 birds at 22.28, Handicap 358 at 19.89 — first in each, and the Singles crown by a single bird over Brad's 400. Eighteen weeks of work decided by one clay. He's on Joe's trophy list, and nobody had to ask why.

The hard stretch. Dave's gun was stolen before W13. What followed was a Handicap run of 12, 11 and then a 6 in W15 that is the lowest card any of us put on paper this year — five stations, mostly O's, checked twice against the card because nobody wanted to believe it. Then the new gun came around: 14, 17, 20. He closed the season shooting his best Handicap since June and his best Singles since W12. That's not luck, that's re-learning a gun in public.

How it ended. W16 a sweep. W17 a split — Singles won 116–112 over Schooner Pub with only five men on the line, Kevin out. W18 a sweep to close: 125–123 Singles, 101–92 Handicap. Three wins in the last four class-nights, and the final card of 2026 was a win in both. Whatever else this summer was, it did not end quietly.

The ledger: 25 beer birds down, all five misses squared, $0 open. Five hangs all year at $25. Two zero-stations. Kitty closes at $272 for the season, $1,005 all-time — Dave leading it at $68, Brad cleanest at $21, which is what three season-highs and a 22.22 will do for a man's wallet.
